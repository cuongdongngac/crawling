## Problem

The original approach: keep re-scanning the hashtag then updating the post's likes and comments. This approach has three problems:

1. **Costly** — every time it re-downloads old posts just to get new like counts.
2. **Losing track of posts** — a post dropping out of the hashtag's search results can no longer be tracked, even though it still exists and still gains engagement.
3. **Uneven data** — whether a post is measured more or less is luck, so a standard curve cannot be drawn.

## New approach: separation of duties

**Find posts (Apify) → Screening (rules + AI + researchers) → Periodic engagement measurement (Social Fetch) → Dashboard, flat data**

Each stage has one tool, one responsibility, one log, and its own cost item. If a stage breaks or needs a provider change, replace that stage; the other stages stay the same.

| | Discovery flow | Engagement flow |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Find **new posts** | Track **existing posts** |
| Input | Approved hashtags/keywords | List of post links in the system |
| Tool | Apify (hashtag-search actor) | **Social Fetch** (measurement by post link); Bright Data / Apify actor as fallback |
| Schedule | Daily, scan effort kept stable | Each day select posts reaching a measurement milestone |
| Result | New posts + first measurement (day 0) | One more metric row per measurement |

- The numbers Apify returns when finding posts are recorded as the **first measurement** (even when empty — it still confirms the post existed at detection). Each measurement knows its source (Apify or Social Fetch); the analysis team decides whether to chain the two sources.
- An existing post seen again by a hashtag scan: no new post added, only a "seen again" record (a signal the post is still being pushed) and the numbers are taken too.
- Turning off a hashtag: posts already collected from that hashtag **are still measured on the full schedule**.
- The list of posts to measure is auto-generated by the system from the data store, not using Apify's fixed schedule.

## Screening before measurement

- AI screens within 24 hours after a post is found.
- **Relevant** or **unclear** posts → still measured normally while awaiting human review (no loss of the start of the curve).
- **Irrelevant** posts → not measured. If the verifier concludes otherwise, adjust: stop measuring (keep old data, exclude from analysis) or add to the measurement schedule from then on.

## Numbers per measurement

Likes (Facebook: total of all reaction types), comments, shares, views (video/reel only), saves (TikTok). Each measurement is a new row, no overwriting.

## A post's measurement schedule (step ladder)

| Phase | When measured |
|---|---|
| First 30 days from **detection** | Day 0 (the scan), 3, 7, 14, 30 |
| Post detected when already over 60 days old | Only day 0 and 30 |
| After 30 days, post **still growing** | Monthly inventory |
| After 30 days, post **cooled down** (growth under 5% between two measurements) | Full inventory before the 4 analysis rounds (months 6, 10, 14, 18) and once right after each new policy milestone |
| Post **disappears** (two consecutive measurements at least 24 hours apart with no sighting) | Record disappearance date, stop measuring |

**Why count from detection, not posting?** A hashtag scan returns posts from many months, many years ago, so "1 hour after posting" cannot be measured. Each measurement still records a timestamp; when analyzing, use the post's **real age** (measurement timestamp minus posting date).

**Why not measure every post every night?** Posts accumulate; after 18 months about 14,000 posts. Measuring all every night costs hundreds of USD per month. The step ladder costs about **20–35 USD/month**, with a cost cap; over the cap, prioritize the first 30-day milestone.

**Why still measure after 30 days?** The project runs 18 months and needs to know whether posts **still exist or have disappeared** around policy milestones.
